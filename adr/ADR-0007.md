# ADR 0007 — Data residency: ap-south-1 primary, ap-south-2 DR

**Status:** Proposed · **Date:** 2026-07-03 · **Confidence:** High
**Phase classification (ADR 0010):** MVP Implementation (deploy in `ap-south-1` — free to do now).
Cross-region DR to `ap-south-2` with RPO/RTO targets is a Production Implementation; multi-country
regions are a Future Enhancement.

## Context
Master Ch. 6 names AWS but no region or residency strategy, while claiming multi-country ambition.
For India v1 with PHI and ABDM participation, keeping data in-country is the defensible posture even
where DPDP does not blanket-mandate localization (the Act permits the government to restrict transfers
to specified countries; health data is high-sensitivity and ABDM expectations favor in-India storage).

## Decision
- **Primary region: AWS Asia Pacific (Mumbai) `ap-south-1`.** DR: **Hyderabad `ap-south-2`.**
- All PHI, backups, audit WORM store, and encryption keys (KMS) remain within Indian regions.
- Define explicit **RPO/RTO** targets per data class (clinical writes get the tightest RPO). Aurora
  cross-region/backup strategy sized to meet them. (Targets to be set in an ops ADR.)
- The residency boundary is modeled as configuration so a future second country is a new region
  deployment, not a re-architecture (supports the multi-country ambition without paying for it now).

## Consequences
- (+) Clean regulatory story for Indian hospitals and ABDM; predictable latency for Indian users.
- (+) Region-pluggable design keeps global expansion open.
- (−) `ap-south-2` has a narrower service catalog than `ap-south-1`; verify every managed service used
  in prod is available there before committing it as DR.
- (−) Cross-region DR adds cost and replication complexity; justified for clinical data criticality.

## Follow-up
Separate ops ADR to fix RPO/RTO numbers, backup cadence, and DR failover runbook.
